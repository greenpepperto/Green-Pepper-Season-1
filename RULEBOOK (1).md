# 🌶️ GREEN PEPPER SEASON 1 — RULEBOOK

## Official Counter-Strike 2 Tournament Rules

**Version:** Season 1  
**Game:** Counter-Strike 2  
**Platform:** FACEIT  
**Anti-Cheat:** FACEIT Anti-Cheat  
**Format:** Online  
**Teams:** 16  
**Prize Pool:** €800  
**Timezone:** GMT / UTC+0

---

# 1. TOURNAMENT FORMAT

Green Pepper Season 1 is a 16-team online Counter-Strike 2 tournament.

## Group Stage

- 2 groups of 8 teams.
- Full Round Robin.
- Each team plays 7 BO1 matches.
- Each group contains 28 matches.
- Top 4 teams from each group advance.
- Bottom 4 teams from each group are eliminated.

## Playoffs

- 8 teams qualify.
- Single Elimination.
- Quarterfinals — BO3.
- Semifinals — BO3.
- Grand Final — BO3.

Total tournament matches:

**63**

# 5. MAP VETO

## BO1

The standard BO1 veto procedure is:

1. Team A bans one map.
2. Team B bans one map.
3. Team A bans one map.
4. Team B bans one map.
5. Team A bans one map.
6. Team B bans one map.
7. The remaining map is played.

Tournament Administration may assign veto order before the match.

## BO3

The standard BO3 veto procedure is:

1. Team A bans one map.
2. Team B bans one map.
3. Team A picks Map 1.
4. Team B picks Map 2.
5. Team A bans one map.
6. Team B bans one map.
7. The remaining map becomes Map 3.

# 6. MATCH SETTINGS

All matches use the official competitive Counter-Strike 2 settings designated by Tournament Administration/FACEIT.

Players must not intentionally modify server settings or interfere with server configuration.

---

# 7. OVERTIME

A match cannot end in a draw.

If regulation ends tied, overtime is played using the tournament's approved competitive overtime configuration.

Overtime continues until a winner is determined.

The same competitive-integrity rules apply during overtime.

---

# 8. TACTICAL PAUSES

Each team receives:

- **3 tactical pauses per map during regulation**
- Each tactical pause lasts **30 seconds**
- **1 additional tactical pause per team during overtime**

Teams must use the approved pause system.

Abuse of the pause system may result in penalties.

---

# 9. TECHNICAL PAUSES

Technical pauses may be requested for legitimate technical problems, including:

- Player crash
- PC issue
- Internet connection failure
- Server problem
- Game client problem
- Anti-Cheat issue
- Serious hardware/software problem

Teams must immediately notify the appropriate administrator.

Technical pauses must not be used to gain an unfair strategic advantage.

---

# 10. PLAYER DISCONNECTS

If a player disconnects:

1. The team must notify administration.
2. The match may be paused where technically possible.
3. The player should reconnect as soon as possible.
4. Administration determines whether the match should continue, be restored, or be otherwise handled.

Repeated or suspicious disconnects may be investigated.

---

# 11. SERVER FAILURE

If a server fails or becomes materially unplayable, Tournament Administration may:

- Restart the server.
- Restore the match where technically possible.
- Replay affected rounds.
- Continue from an approved state.
- Restart the map.
- Reschedule the match.

The decision depends on the circumstances and available evidence.

---

# 12. ROUND RESTORATION

Round restoration may be considered when:

- A server failure materially affects the game.
- A major technical issue occurs.
- An administrative error affects competitive integrity.

Administration may use available server/demo information when determining the appropriate restoration point.

---

# 13. BREAKS BETWEEN MAPS

In BO3 matches, a short administrative break may occur between maps.

Teams must remain available.

The exact duration can change depending on:

- Technical issues
- Server preparation
- Broadcast requirements
- Administration
- Previous map duration

# 14. CHEATING & ANTI-CHEAT

Cheating is strictly prohibited.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

- Aimbot
- Wallhack
- Triggerbot
- Radar hacks
- Recoil cheats
- Unauthorized game modifications
- Memory manipulation
- DLL/injector-based cheats
- Hardware-assisted cheating
- Any software providing an unfair competitive advantage

Players must comply with FACEIT Anti-Cheat requirements.

A suspected cheat may be investigated even after a match has ended.

---

# 15. UNAUTHORIZED SOFTWARE

Players may not use software that provides an unfair advantage.

Tournament Administration may require players to:

- Disable prohibited applications.
- Provide relevant evidence.
- Complete additional Anti-Cheat checks.
- Cooperate with an investigation.

Refusal to cooperate may result in penalties.

---

# 16. BUGS & EXPLOITS

Intentional abuse of game-breaking bugs or exploits is prohibited.

Players must report serious competitive bugs to administration.

Using a bug to obtain an unfair advantage may result in:

- Round loss
- Map loss
- Match loss
- Disqualification
- Further disciplinary action

---

Players may not obtain live information about their own match from:

- Streams
- Spectators
- Coaches outside the approved communication structure
- Friends
- Opposing players
- Social media
- Other external sources

# 18. COACHING

Coaching is only permitted when explicitly allowed by the Tournament Administration.

# 19. PLAYER SETTINGS & CONFIGURATION

Players may use legitimate in-game settings and configurations permitted by Counter-Strike 2 and FACEIT.

# 36. GROUP STAGE TIEBREAKERS

Tiebreakers are applied in this order:

1. **Match Record**
2. **Head-to-Head Result**
3. **Round Difference**
4. **Total Rounds Won**
5. **Strength of Schedule**
6. **Additional Tiebreak Match**

Round Difference:

`Rounds Won − Rounds Lost`

For three-way or larger ties, the criteria are applied to the tied teams as a group. Once one position is resolved, the remaining tied teams may be re-evaluated.

The tiebreaker system is particularly important for **4th place**, which determines Playoff qualification.

If a tiebreak match is required, Administration will announce the time and format.

Failure to participate in a required tiebreak match may result in a default loss or elimination.

# 38. SCHEDULE RULES

All official tournament times use **GMT / UTC+0**.

Green Pepper Season 1 uses a Rolling Schedule.

Published times are target times and may change because of:

- Match duration
- Overtime
- Technical pauses
- Disconnects
- Server issues
- Protests
- Administration

Teams must remain available during their assigned competition window.

The tournament is designed to avoid intentionally forcing players into fixed midnight matches, while exceptional match circumstances may still cause delays.

---

# 39. PLAYER-FRIENDLY SCHEDULING

The competition schedule is designed around the reality that many players have work, studies and other commitments.

The tournament therefore provides:

- Weekend Group Stage matches
- A five-day competitive break before Playoffs
- Rolling scheduling
- Recovery time between Playoff series
- Target rather than artificially rigid match times

Players and teams remain responsible for being available for their assigned match windows.
