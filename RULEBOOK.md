## 1. Registration Regulations

### 1.1 Registration and Entry Fee

- **1.1.1** Teams must register through the official Green Pepper registration process announced by the organizers.
- **1.1.2** Green Pepper Season 1 is an open-registration online European CS2 tournament. VRS rankings are used for eligibility assessment, seeding, and direct playoff qualification; they are not invitations to participate.
- **1.1.3** The entry fee is **€250 per team**. Payment instructions and deadlines will be communicated through official Green Pepper channels.
- **1.1.4** A registration is not considered complete until all required registration details have been submitted and payment has been confirmed.
- **1.1.5** Entry fees are non-refundable once a team has been accepted, except where Green Pepper administration announces otherwise or applicable law requires otherwise.
- **1.1.6** The tournament is limited to **24 accepted teams**. The organizers may close registration once all slots are filled.
- **1.1.7** A team captain must be designated during registration. A captain change must be reported to administration; the replacement captain must be on the team's approved roster.
- **1.1.8** Teams must provide accurate team, player, Steam, and FACEIT information. False or misleading information may result in rejection or disqualification.

### 1.2 Roster Submission

- **1.2.1** Teams must register five starting players and may register substitutes and a coach, subject to the roster limits and registration process announced by Green Pepper.
- **1.2.2** Teams must submit each player's display name, Steam account details, and FACEIT profile.
- **1.2.3** Players may represent only one team in the tournament. Multi-accounting, account sharing, and playing for multiple teams are prohibited.
- **1.2.4** Players must use their own accounts and must not allow another person to play on their behalf.
- **1.2.5** Administration may request reasonable evidence to verify a player's identity, account ownership, roster eligibility, or VRS match.
- **1.2.6** Roster changes require administration approval where specified in this Rulebook. Approval does not waive the fixed-reference-roster requirements in Section 1.3.

### 1.3 Roster Limitations and Core Policy

#### 1.3.1 Fixed five-player reference roster

The five-player reference roster is fixed for roster-continuity checks:

- **VRS-matched teams:** the five players on the team's matched official VRS roster in the **2 November 2026 VRS snapshot**.
- **Non-VRS teams:** the five original starters submitted in the team's accepted registration.

Teams must field at least **three of the five players** from their applicable reference roster for the entirety of every match, including the Swiss Stage and Playoffs. The qualifying three may differ from match to match; teams do not nominate three permanently fixed core players.

#### 1.3.2 VRS-matched teams

- A team is considered VRS-matched when at least three of its five registered starting players match the same official five-player VRS roster in the applicable snapshot. Substitutes are not counted for the initial matching decision.
- VRS-matched teams must field at least three players from that matched VRS five-player roster throughout every match.
- Teams must maintain at least three players from the matched VRS roster on their official tournament roster throughout the event. Falling below three may result in disqualification.
- Approved substitutions or roster changes do not waive the three-of-five requirement.

#### 1.3.3 Non-VRS teams

- Non-VRS teams must field at least three of their five original registered starters throughout every match.
- Later roster additions do not become original starters and do not change the reference roster.
- The qualifying three original starters may differ between matches.

#### 1.3.4 Roster size and coaches

The permitted roster size and registration slots will be stated in the official registration instructions. Teams must not field unregistered players. A coach may substitute as a player only if listed on the official roster before the match, eligible to participate, and the lineup continues to satisfy the applicable three-of-five requirement.

#### 1.3.5 Duplicate cores

No two teams may use a shared core of three or more players from their applicable five-player reference rosters. Administration may investigate suspected duplicate cores and may reject, reseed, or disqualify teams where necessary to protect competitive integrity.

#### 1.3.6 Roster changes

- **Before and during the Swiss Stage:** roster changes may be requested, subject to administration approval. Approved changes do not alter the fixed five-player reference roster.
- **During the Playoffs:** teams may make no more than **two substitute-slot roster changes in total** across the Playoffs, limited to the Sub 1 / Sub 2 slots and subject to administration approval.
- Replacing one player in either substitute slot counts as one change. Replacing both substitute-slot players counts as two changes.
- Using a substitute already listed on the approved roster does not itself count as a substitute-slot roster change.
- Every lineup must continue to meet the applicable three-of-five requirement.

### 1.4 Substitutions

- **1.4.1 Between matches:** teams may use an approved, registered substitute, provided the lineup meets all eligibility and roster-continuity requirements.
- **1.4.2 During a match or between maps:** substitutions are permitted only in a verifiable emergency and require prior approval from tournament administration where circumstances allow.
- **1.4.3** The captain must contact administration through the official Green Pepper support channel as soon as an emergency substitution is required.
- **1.4.4** Administration may deny a substitution if it would violate the roster rules, materially disrupt the match, or undermine competitive integrity.

## 2. Tournament Format and Structure

### 2.1 Tournament Overview

- **2.1.1 Game:** Counter-Strike 2.
- **2.1.2 Region:** Europe.
- **2.1.3 Competition type:** online Tier-2 tournament, VRS-aligned.
- **2.1.4 Platform:** FACEIT.
- **2.1.5 Accepted teams:** 24.
- **2.1.6 Entry fee:** €250 per team.
- **2.1.7 Total advertised prize pool:** €5,000.
- **2.1.8 Format:** eight eligible VRS-ranked teams qualify directly for the 16-team Playoffs; the remaining 16 teams contest a Swiss Stage for the eight remaining playoff places.
- **2.1.9** Match dates, round windows, and start times are published in the official [Schedule](SCHEDULE.md) and may be clarified through official Green Pepper announcements.

### 2.2 VRS Direct Playoff Qualification

- **2.2.1** Eight teams qualify directly for the Playoffs based on the official VRS ranking snapshot dated **2 November 2026**.
- **2.2.2** Direct places are awarded to the eight highest-ranked eligible VRS teams among accepted registered entrants.
- **2.2.3** Direct qualification is a result of the published VRS ranking and the event's open-registration format; it is not a direct invitation to the tournament.
- **2.2.4** A team must be registered, accepted, and eligible to receive a direct playoff place.
- **2.2.5** If a team with a potential direct place is not registered or is ruled ineligible, the place passes to the next-highest-ranked eligible registered team.
- **2.2.6** Green Pepper will publish the VRS snapshot date and the list of direct qualifiers before the Swiss Stage begins.
- **2.2.7** VRS-matched teams remain subject to the roster-continuity rules in Section 1.3.

### 2.3 Swiss Stage

- **2.3.1** The Swiss Stage consists of **16 teams**: eligible registered teams that do not receive a direct VRS playoff place.
- **2.3.2** All Swiss Stage matches are **Best-of-Three (BO3)**.
- **2.3.3** A team qualifies for the Playoffs after recording **three match wins**.
- **2.3.4** A team is eliminated after recording **three match losses**.
- **2.3.5** Eight teams qualify from the Swiss Stage to join the eight VRS direct qualifiers in the 16-team Playoff bracket.
- **2.3.6** Teams are paired with opponents on the same or a comparable match record, subject to the pairing method announced by administration.
- **2.3.7** Teams should not face the same opponent more than once in the Swiss Stage unless an unavoidable bracket or administrative issue requires an exception.
- **2.3.8** Swiss pairings, seeding, match windows, and applicable tiebreak procedures will be published before the stage begins.
- **2.3.9** If a team withdraws or is disqualified, administration determines how its results and remaining pairings are handled, aiming to preserve competitive fairness.

### 2.4 Playoffs

- **2.4.1** The Playoffs consist of **16 teams** in a single-elimination bracket.
- **2.4.2** The bracket contains eight VRS direct qualifiers and eight Swiss Stage qualifiers.
- **2.4.3** Standard playoff matches are **BO3**.
- **2.4.4** The Grand Final is **Best-of-Five (BO5)**.
- **2.4.5** A team is eliminated after losing a playoff match.
- **2.4.6** The playoff bracket and seeding method will be published by administration after Swiss qualification is complete.
- **2.4.7** The Playoffs remain subject to the roster-change limits and three-of-five reference-roster rule in Section 1.

### 2.5 Seeding and Tiebreakers

#### 2.5.1 Seeding order

1. Eligible VRS-matched teams are ordered by rank in the fixed **2 November 2026 VRS snapshot**.
2. Remaining teams are seeded using documented competitive results and any criteria announced by Green Pepper before the draw.
3. Teams without a comparable VRS ranking or competitive record are assessed using the documented information available to administration.
4. Final seeding and the method used will be published before the relevant stage begins.

#### 2.5.2 Swiss standings

- Swiss standings are primarily determined by match record.
- Teams with the same record are separated using the published Swiss pairing and tiebreak rules.
- If a tie remains unresolved by the published criteria, administration may use match results between tied teams where applicable, strength of opponents, map/round differential, or another announced objective metric.
- Administration must apply the same criteria consistently and may request match records or other evidence to resolve a standings issue.
- Teams must not assume that head-to-head is the sole tiebreaker unless the official stage-specific rules explicitly state so.

#### 2.5.3 Administrative corrections

If an incorrect result, ineligible player, forfeit, or other integrity issue affects standings, administration may correct the standings and adjust qualification or seeding accordingly. Affected teams will be informed through official channels.

## 3. Online Match Rules

### 3.1 Platform Requirements

- **3.1.1** All official matches must be played through the designated FACEIT tournament infrastructure or another match service explicitly announced by Green Pepper.
- **3.1.2** Every player must have a valid FACEIT account and join the correct match room with the registered account.
- **3.1.3** Match results, match links, brackets, and standings are tracked through the official FACEIT tournament setup and/or the channels designated by administration.
- **3.1.4** Players must follow match-room instructions and must not start, abandon, or alter an official match without authorization.
- **3.1.5** A team that cannot access its match room must notify administration immediately and provide evidence of the issue.

### 3.2 Anti-Cheat and Eligibility

- **3.2.1 FACEIT Anti-Cheat is mandatory** for every player in official matches.
- **3.2.2** Players must have Anti-Cheat running whenever required by FACEIT and tournament administration.
- **3.2.3** A match may not begin if a player fails to meet the Anti-Cheat requirement. Administration may issue a forfeit if the issue is not resolved within the allowed start window.
- **3.2.4** Cheating, unauthorized third-party software, account sharing, and attempts to bypass Anti-Cheat are prohibited.
- **3.2.5** Players with active bans that make them ineligible under FACEIT rules or the event's announced eligibility policy may not participate.
- **3.2.6** If a player is banned or found ineligible during the tournament, administration may investigate the team's previous results and impose penalties, including forfeits or disqualification, where justified.
- **3.2.7** Players must not use another person's account or allow another person to play for them.

### 3.3 Match Settings

The official FACEIT server configuration takes precedence where it differs from this summary. Unless Green Pepper announces otherwise before a match, the intended competitive settings are:

- **Game:** Counter-Strike 2.
- **Regulation format:** MR12.
- **Regulation start money:** $800.
- **Freeze time:** 15 seconds.
- **Round time:** 1 minute 55 seconds.
- **Overtime:** MR6, with $12,500 starting money per team.
- **Overtime continuation:** teams change sides according to the server's configured overtime rules and continue overtime sets until a winner is determined.
- **Custom player models or unauthorized cosmetic modifications that provide an unfair advantage are prohibited.**

Administration will clarify any server-side setting that differs from this section. Players must not change server settings themselves.

### 3.4 Map Veto

- **3.4.1 BO3 veto:** Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → the remaining map is the decider.
- **3.4.2** Vetoes should be completed immediately before the scheduled match, using the official match room or administration instructions.
- **3.4.3** The higher-seeded team is Team A and chooses whether to act first or second, unless the match platform or a published stage-specific rule specifies otherwise.
- **3.4.4** If a team fails to complete its veto on time, administration may make the remaining veto decisions on its behalf.
- **3.4.5 BO5 Grand Final veto:** Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → the remaining map is the final map.
- **3.4.6** The Grand Final is a standard BO5 unless Green Pepper publishes a different format before the Playoffs. There is no automatic map advantage unless the organizers explicitly announce one in the official bracket rules.
- **3.4.7** The official active map pool will be announced before the tournament stage. Administration may update it if CS2 or the competition platform changes the available map pool.

### 3.5 Servers and Region

- **3.5.1** Green Pepper Season 1 is an online European event.
- **3.5.2** Matches use the server selected by FACEIT or designated by administration for that match.
- **3.5.3** Players are responsible for ensuring their internet connection, hardware, and software are suitable for online competition.
- **3.5.4** A team must report a server-location or routing concern before the match where possible. Administration determines whether a server change is appropriate.
- **3.5.5** A player's poor connection alone does not automatically entitle the team to a reschedule or replay.

### 3.6 Integrity Verification

- **3.6.1** Players must cooperate with reasonable integrity checks requested by tournament administration.
- **3.6.2** Administration may request players to share their screen, provide relevant recordings, or complete other reasonable verification steps where permitted by platform rules and applicable law.
- **3.6.3** A refusal to cooperate with a reasonable and properly communicated integrity check may lead to further investigation or disciplinary action.
- **3.6.4** Any camera requirement will be communicated in advance or explained by administration when a specific integrity concern arises; it is not an automatic requirement unless officially announced.

## 4. Scheduling

### 4.1 Match Scheduling

- **4.1.1** The official schedule is published in `SCHEDULE.md` and through Green Pepper's official channels.
- **4.1.2** All displayed times use the time zone specified in the official schedule. If no time zone is stated, teams must ask administration rather than assume.
- **4.1.3** Teams are responsible for checking their match windows, confirming match arrangements, and being available during the announced period.
- **4.1.4** Captains must coordinate through the official match chat or designated Green Pepper communication channel.
- **4.1.5** A team must not assume that a proposed time is approved until the opponent and, where required, administration have confirmed it.

### 4.2 Rescheduling

- **4.2.1** Reschedule requests must be made in the official match chat or support channel and must include the match link, reason, and proposed alternative times.
- **4.2.2** During the Swiss Stage, a reschedule is valid only if it stays within the published round window and complies with the official schedule.
- **4.2.3** Playoff reschedules require administration approval.
- **4.2.4** Administration may deny a reschedule that would delay the tournament, create an unfair competitive advantage, or conflict with later matches.
- **4.2.5** Teams must not use rescheduling to avoid an opponent, gain additional preparation time unfairly, or delay a result.
- **4.2.6** If teams cannot agree on a time, they must contact administration before the match deadline. Administration may set a mandatory match time.

### 4.3 Punctuality and Forfeits

- **4.3.1** Teams must be ready to play at the confirmed match start time, with eligible players available and Anti-Cheat functioning.
- **4.3.2** A **15-minute grace period** applies unless the official schedule or an administrator states otherwise.
- **4.3.3** A team that cannot field a complete eligible lineup after the grace period may receive a forfeit loss.
- **4.3.4** A match not completed within its round window without an approved reschedule may be recorded as a forfeit.
- **4.3.5** Administration may consider documented platform outages or exceptional circumstances before confirming a forfeit.
- **4.3.6** Teams must provide evidence promptly when disputing a no-show or forfeit decision.

## 5. Technical Issues

### 5.1 Pauses and Timeouts

- **5.1.1 Technical pauses** are for genuine technical issues such as a game crash, server problem, or player disconnection. Teams must explain the reason when requested.
- **5.1.2** Each team may request up to one technical timeout of up to 15 minutes per match, unless FACEIT's match configuration or a published event-specific rule provides otherwise.
- **5.1.3 Tactical pauses are limited to the number and duration supported by the official match configuration. If the platform does not enforce a published allowance, captains must request pauses through the approved method.
- **5.1.4** Teams must not use technical pauses to gain an unfair tactical advantage, disrupt an opponent, or delay a match without cause.
- **5.1.5** Administration may shorten, extend, or deny a pause based on the circumstances and tournament schedule.

### 5.2 Disconnections

- **5.2.1** If a player disconnects, the affected team must use the in-game pause feature where available and notify the opponent and administration as soon as possible.
- **5.2.2** Teams must not resume play until the issue has been addressed according to the server rules and any administrator instructions.
- **5.2.3** The response to a disconnection depends on the match state, server capability, platform rules, and whether the issue affects one or both teams.
- **5.2.4** A replay or rollback is not automatic. Administration decides whether a replay, continuation, round restart, or other remedy is appropriate.
- **5.2.5** Repeated or unexplained disconnections may result in a forfeit if a team cannot complete the match.

### 5.3 Game Updates

- **5.3.1** If a CS2 update or platform maintenance prevents the match from running properly, teams must pause where possible and contact administration.
- **5.3.2** Teams must not independently restart or reschedule an affected official match without approval.
- **5.3.3** Administration will decide whether to delay, reschedule, restart, or continue the match based on platform status and competitive fairness.

### 5.4 Internet and Hardware Issues

- **5.4.1** Players are responsible for their own internet connection, PC, peripherals, drivers, and software.
- **5.4.2** A personal power, hardware, or ISP issue does not automatically require a replay or reschedule.
- **5.4.3** Teams must report a major connection or platform-wide issue immediately and provide evidence where possible.
- **5.4.4** Administration may grant an exception when a documented, widespread technical incident materially affects the match and a remedy is necessary to protect competitive integrity.

## 6. Broadcast and Media

### 6.1 Player Streaming

- **6.1.1** Players may stream their own point of view unless Green Pepper administration announces a restriction for a specific match.
- **6.1.2** Player POV streams must use at least a **120-second delay** if required by administration to protect match integrity.
- **6.1.3** Stream titles should identify Green Pepper Season 1 where practical and must not falsely imply that a personal stream is an official Green Pepper broadcast.
- **6.1.4** Streamers must retain a recording or VOD if requested by administration for a protest or integrity review.
- **6.1.5** Streaming permission does not allow players or coaches to watch a live broadcast of their own ongoing match or receive outside information about it.
- **6.1.6** Administration may require a stream to be delayed, modified, or stopped if it creates a competitive integrity or rights issue.

### 6.2 Community Broadcasting

- **6.2.1** Community casters who wish to broadcast matches must request permission through the official Green Pepper contact channel before broadcasting.
- **6.2.2** Approved broadcasters must follow any technical, delay, branding, and conduct conditions provided by administration.
- **6.2.3** Participation in the tournament or access to a match room does not automatically grant broadcast permission.
- **6.2.4** Green Pepper may approve, restrict, or revoke community broadcast permission to protect competitive integrity or official broadcast arrangements.

### 6.3 Spectator Access

- **6.3.1** Spectator or GOTV/CSTV access may be granted only through the approved method and at administration's discretion.
- **6.3.2** Passwords, server addresses, and restricted spectator details must not be shared with unauthorized persons.
- **6.3.3** Spectator access does not authorize a person to rebroadcast the match or share live competitive information with a team.
- **6.3.4** Administration may revoke spectator access if it is misused.

### 6.4 Official Broadcast Rights

- **6.4.1** Green Pepper may broadcast selected official matches itself or through approved partners.
- **6.4.2** Official broadcast delays and production conditions will be set by Green Pepper and communicated to broadcasters.
- **6.4.3** Re-streaming or retransmitting an official Green Pepper or partner broadcast requires prior written permission.
- **6.4.4** Permissions to stream a player's own POV or community-cast a match do not automatically permit use of the official broadcast feed.
- **6.4.5** Broadcast violations may result in a stream being stopped, access being revoked, or further penalties where appropriate.

### 6.5 Co-Streaming

- **6.5.1** Co-streaming an official Green Pepper broadcast requires prior written approval.
- **6.5.2** Approved co-streamers must follow all conditions provided by Green Pepper, including branding, delay, and content restrictions.
- **6.5.3** Co-stream approval does not automatically grant independent spectator access or permission to produce a separate community broadcast.
- **6.5.4** Green Pepper may deny or revoke co-stream permission to protect rights holders, competitive integrity, or event operations.

## 7. Penalties and Prohibited Conduct

Cheating, account sharing, stream sniping, match manipulation, harassment, and other integrity violations may result in penalties up to and including disqualification. Administration considers the evidence, severity, intent, competitive impact, and prior violations.

### 7.1 Cheating and Third-Party Software

- **7.1.1** Cheating of any kind is prohibited and may result in immediate disqualification.
- **7.1.2** Unauthorized third-party software, scripts, hardware, or modifications that provide an unfair advantage are prohibited.
- **7.1.3** Account sharing, impersonation, and allowing an unregistered player to compete are prohibited.
- **7.1.4** Attempting to bypass FACEIT Anti-Cheat or an integrity check is a serious violation.
- **7.1.5** Custom models or unauthorized files that provide a competitive advantage are prohibited.
- **7.1.6** If a player is found to have cheated or becomes ineligible during the event, administration may review the team's results and impose forfeits or disqualification where justified.

### 7.2 Exploits and Illegal Actions

- **7.2.1** Deliberately exploiting a game bug for an unfair advantage is prohibited.
- **7.2.2** Pixel walking, use of invisible or unintended ledges, and other prohibited map exploits are not allowed where they violate CS2 competitive rules or event instructions.
- **7.2.3** Players must not use unintended bomb-plant locations or other glitches to make the objective inaccessible or unfair.
- **7.2.4** Players must not intentionally join the opposing team or manipulate team assignment to gain an advantage.
- **7.2.5** Boosting or clipping through solid geometry to gain an illegal position is prohibited.
- **7.2.6** Players must report a serious unintended exploit to administration rather than continue abusing it.
- **7.2.7** Penalties depend on the nature and impact of the violation and may include a round loss, map loss, match forfeit, or disqualification.

### 7.3 Communication and Behaviour

- **7.3.1** Players, coaches, and team representatives must treat opponents, admins, casters, and other participants respectfully.
- **7.3.2** Racist, discriminatory, threatening, or harassing conduct is prohibited.
- **7.3.3** Serious discriminatory abuse may result in a match forfeit, removal from the event, or a tournament ban. Repeated offences may lead to stronger penalties.
- **7.3.4** Threats, doxxing, targeted harassment, and attempts to intimidate an opponent or administrator are serious violations.
- **7.3.5** Publicly sharing private administrative discussions, confidential investigation material, or private personal information without authorization may result in penalties.
- **7.3.6** All participants must follow reasonable instructions from tournament administration and uphold fair play.

### 7.4 Stream Sniping and Outside Information

- **7.4.1** Players and coaches must not watch a live broadcast of their own ongoing match unless administration expressly authorizes it.
- **7.4.2** This restriction applies between maps and during technical pauses.
- **7.4.3** Teams must not receive live information from spectators, casters, friends, or other outside sources.
- **7.4.4** Suspected stream sniping or outside communication will be investigated using available evidence.
- **7.4.5** Proven violations may result in a round or match loss, disqualification, or a ban.

### 7.5 Betting and Match Manipulation

- **7.5.1** Players, coaches, and team representatives must not bet on their own matches or use confidential tournament information for betting.
- **7.5.2** Match-fixing, intentionally losing, collusion, result manipulation, or attempts to influence another team to manipulate a match are prohibited.
- **7.5.3** Participants must report credible approaches involving match manipulation to tournament administration.
- **7.5.4** Violations may result in immediate disqualification, prize forfeiture, and referral to the relevant platform or competition integrity body where appropriate.

### 7.6 Repeated Violations

- **7.6.1** Repeated violations may result in escalating penalties.
- **7.6.2** Administration may remove a team whose conduct repeatedly disrupts the event or undermines competitive integrity.
- **7.6.3** A team is responsible for the conduct of its players, coach, and representatives participating in event activities.
- **7.6.4** Penalties may include a warning, round loss, map loss, match forfeit, disqualification, prize forfeiture, or exclusion from future Green Pepper events.

## 8. Tournament Administration

### 8.1 Administrative Authority

- **8.1.1** Green Pepper administration interprets and enforces this Rulebook.

## 7. Penalties and Prohibited Conduct

Cheating, account sharing, stream sniping, match manipulation, harassment, and other integrity violations may result in penalties up to and including disqualification. Administration considers the evidence, severity, intent, competitive impact, and prior violations.

### 7.1 Cheating and Third-Party Software

- **7.1.1** Cheating of any kind is prohibited and may result in immediate disqualification.
- **7.1.2** Unauthorized third-party software, scripts, hardware, or modifications that provide an unfair advantage are prohibited.
- **7.1.3** Account sharing, impersonation, and allowing an unregistered player to compete are prohibited.
- **7.1.4** Attempting to bypass FACEIT Anti-Cheat or an integrity check is a serious violation.
- **7.1.5** Custom models or unauthorized files that provide a competitive advantage are prohibited.
- **7.1.6** If a player is found to have cheated or becomes ineligible during the event, administration may review the team's results and impose forfeits or disqualification where justified.

### 7.2 Exploits and Illegal Actions

- **7.2.1** Deliberately exploiting a game bug for an unfair advantage is prohibited.
- **7.2.2** Pixel walking, use of invisible or unintended ledges, and other prohibited map exploits are not allowed where they violate CS2 competitive rules or event instructions.
- **7.2.3** Players must not use unintended bomb-plant locations or glitches to make the objective inaccessible or unfair.
- **7.2.4** Players must not intentionally join the opposing team or manipulate team assignment to gain an advantage.
- **7.2.5** Boosting or clipping through solid geometry to gain an illegal position is prohibited.
- **7.2.6** Players must report a serious unintended exploit to administration rather than continue abusing it.
- **7.2.7** Penalties depend on the nature and impact of the violation and may include a round loss, map loss, match forfeit, or disqualification.

### 7.3 Communication and Behaviour

- **7.3.1** Players, coaches, and team representatives must treat opponents, admins, casters, and other participants respectfully.
- **7.3.2** Racist, discriminatory, threatening, or harassing conduct is prohibited.

- **7.3.4** Threats, doxxing, targeted harassment, and attempts to intimidate an opponent or administrator are serious violations.
- **7.3.5** Publicly sharing private administrative discussions, confidential investigation material, or private personal information without authorization may result in penalties.
- **7.3.6** All participants must follow reasonable instructions from tournament administration and uphold fair play.

### 7.4 Stream Sniping and Outside Information

- **7.4.1** Players and coaches must not watch a live broadcast of their own ongoing match unless administration expressly authorizes it.
- **7.4.2** This restriction applies between maps and during technical pauses.
- **7.4.3** Teams must not receive live information from spectators, casters, friends, or other outside sources.
- **7.4.4** Suspected stream sniping or outside communication will be investigated using available evidence.
- **7.4.5** Proven violations may result in a round or match loss, disqualification, or a ban.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do all players need a FACEIT account and Anti-Cheat?**  
A: Yes. Every player must use their own eligible FACEIT account and have FACEIT Anti-Cheat running for official matches.

**Q: How does VRS qualification work?**  
A: Eight eligible, registered teams with the highest rankings in the fixed 2 November 2026 VRS snapshot receive direct places in the Playoffs. These are direct playoff qualifications within an open-registration tournament, not invitations to participate.

**Q: What happens to teams that do not receive a direct VRS playoff place?**  
A: Sixteen teams compete in the BO3 Swiss Stage. A team qualifies after three wins and is eliminated after three losses. Eight Swiss teams advance to the Playoffs.

**Q: What counts as a team's core?**  
A: For a VRS-matched team, the reference roster is the five players on its matched VRS roster in the 2 November 2026 snapshot. For a non-VRS team, it is the five original registered starters. At least three players from the applicable reference roster must play the entirety of every match.

**Q: Can we change our roster?**  
A: Roster changes require administration approval where specified and do not change the fixed reference roster. During the Playoffs, up to two changes to the Sub 1 / Sub 2 slots are permitted in total, subject to approval.

**Q: Does using an already registered substitute count as a roster change?**  
A: Simply fielding a substitute already listed on the approved roster does not count as a substitute-slot roster change. Every lineup must still satisfy the applicable three-of-five requirement.

**Q: Can a player be substituted during a match?**  
A: Only in a verifiable emergency and subject to administration approval.

**Q: What if a player disconnects?**  
A: Pause where the platform allows, notify the opponent and administration immediately, and follow their instructions. A replay is not automatic.

**Q: Can I stream my POV or cast a match?**  
A: Player POV streaming may be allowed subject to integrity conditions. Community broadcasting and co-streaming require prior approval from Green Pepper.

**Q: How do I submit a protest?**  
A: Use the official Green Pepper support channel and submit evidence. The standard deadline is 24 hours after the match concludes unless a different deadline is announced.

**Q: Who makes the final decision on an issue not covered here?**  
A: Green Pepper tournament administration, applying the principles of competitive integrity and fairness.
